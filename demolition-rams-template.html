Demolition RAMS Template

What a demolition method statement and risk assessment needs to cover, and how CDM 2015 applies, with an editable Word RAMS template.

Why demolition needs a written plan

Demolition and dismantling carry some of the highest risks on any site: unplanned collapse, falls from height, falling materials, hidden asbestos, live services, dust, noise and vibration. The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM 2015) deal with this directly. Regulation 20 says demolition or dismantling must be planned and carried out to prevent danger or, where that isn't practicable, to reduce it as low as reasonably practicable, and that the arrangements must be recorded in writing before the work begins.

In practice that written record is usually a demolition method statement and risk assessment, often called a RAMS, which also feeds into the construction phase plan.

What a demolition RAMS should cover

  • Scope and sequence: what is being demolished, what is staying, and the order of work, so that the structure stays stable at every stage.
  • Surveys and information: the structural survey, drawings, pre-construction information and any temporary works design needed to support retained structures or neighbouring buildings.
  • Asbestos: confirmation that a refurbishment and demolition survey has been carried out and any asbestos has been dealt with before demolition starts.
  • Services: how gas, electricity, water, drainage and telecoms will be identified, isolated and confirmed dead or diverted.
  • Exclusion zones and public protection: hoarding, signage, banksmen, controlled access, and protection of footpaths and roads.
  • Plant and method: machines, attachments, hand demolition, and how operators will be kept clear of the drop zone.
  • Work at height: access equipment, edge protection and fall prevention.
  • Health hazards: dust (including silica), noise, vibration and hazardous substances, with controls such as water suppression and RPE.
  • Waste: segregation, storage and removal of materials.
  • Emergencies: unplanned collapse, injury, fire, and service strikes.
  • Briefing and sign-off: a record that each operative has read and understood the RAMS.

Asbestos comes first

Under the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012, asbestos must be identified before work that could disturb it. For demolition, HSE guidance (HSG264) describes the refurbishment and demolition survey that is normally needed. Any asbestos found should be removed, by a licensed contractor where the work is licensable, before the structure is demolished. Your RAMS should reference the survey and confirm the removal is complete.

Relevant standards and duties

BS 6187, the code of practice for full and partial demolition, is the main British Standard for planning and managing demolition work and is widely referenced in method statements. Other legislation commonly relevant includes the Work at Height Regulations 2005, the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH) for dust, and the Control of Noise at Work and Control of Vibration at Work Regulations.

Under CDM 2015, a project must be notified to HSE (on form F10) if it is expected to last more than 30 working days with more than 20 workers on site at any one time, or to exceed 500 person-days. Larger demolition jobs often meet those thresholds.

Making a template site-specific

A template gives you the structure and a sensible starting list of hazards and controls. It doesn't replace the planning itself. Before use, add the site details, the actual sequence, the survey findings, the plant you will use, and the names of the people responsible. Then brief it to the team and keep the signed record. If the method changes on site, revise the RAMS and brief it again. Principal contractors will normally want to review a demolition RAMS before work starts, so allow time for comments and resubmission.
