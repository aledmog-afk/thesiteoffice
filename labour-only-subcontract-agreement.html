Labour-Only Subcontract Agreement

What a labour-only subcontract should cover, from CIS and employment status to payment terms, with editable Word agreements.

What is a labour-only subcontract?

A labour-only subcontract is an agreement where a self-employed tradesperson or a small firm supplies labour (and usually their own hand tools), while the contractor supplies the materials and most of the plant. It's one of the most common arrangements in UK construction, and also one of the most commonly left unwritten. A short written agreement agreed before work starts avoids most arguments about rates, hours, payment and who is responsible for what. It also gives you a single place to record the details you will need for CIS and for your site records.

What the agreement should cover

  • The parties: full names, addresses, and the subcontractor's UTR and CIS status.
  • The works: the project, location and a clear description of the labour being supplied.
  • Rate and basis: day rate, hourly rate or price for the work, and how extra work will be agreed and recorded.
  • Payment terms: how often payments fall due, how applications are made, and the final date for payment.
  • Tools, materials and plant: who provides what.
  • Health and safety: site induction, following the RAMS and site rules, reporting accidents, and any competence cards the site requires.
  • Insurance: what cover the subcontractor must hold.
  • Defects: putting right their own defective work.
  • Status: a statement of the intended self-employed relationship (see below).
  • Termination and disputes: notice periods, and how disagreements will be resolved.

CIS: verify and deduct correctly

Under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS), a contractor paying a subcontractor for construction work must normally verify the subcontractor with HMRC before paying them. HMRC then confirms the deduction rate: 20% for registered subcontractors, 30% if they aren't registered, or no deduction if they have gross payment status. Deductions are taken from the labour part of the payment, not from materials, and contractors file monthly CIS returns. Record the verification reference and rate in your records.

Employment status: the paperwork isn't the whole story

Calling someone self-employed in a contract doesn't make them self-employed. HMRC, employment tribunals and courts look at the reality of the working relationship: how much control the contractor has over how, when and where the work is done; whether the worker can send a substitute; whether they carry financial risk; whose equipment they use; and whether there's an ongoing obligation to offer and accept work. HMRC's Check Employment Status for Tax (CEST) tool can help with the tax side.

If someone is in reality an employee or a worker, they may be entitled to rights such as holiday pay and the National Minimum Wage, whatever the paperwork says. Where an operative works through their own limited company, the off-payroll working rules (IR35) may also need considering. A written agreement is evidence of what was intended, so make sure it reflects how the job will actually run.

The Construction Act applies too

Contracts to provide labour for construction operations are generally construction contracts under the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, so the Act's payment and adjudication rules apply. That includes payment notices and pay less notices, and either party's right to refer a dispute to adjudication. Setting clear due dates and final dates for payment in the agreement makes this much easier to manage. See our pay less notice guide.

Which template do I need?

We have three agreements. The Labour Only Agreement records both parties' details, UTR, NI and CIS status, the works, rate and payment terms, with terms covering status, supply of labour, control, health and safety, insurance, tools, payment and termination. This page is general guidance, not legal advice. Check the terms of your own contract and take professional advice if a dispute is likely or the sums are significant.
